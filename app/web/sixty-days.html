The human problem

The deadline is simple. The evidence hunt is not.

A decision letter can ask for records held by several different people while the applicant is still recovering from the disaster.

One letter, several obligations

The reason, requested documents, and deadline are mixed into formal language. Missing the exact reason means gathering the wrong evidence.

Evidence lives elsewhere

Ownership, occupancy, insurance, and damage records may come from different offices. The applicant remains responsible for requesting and sending them.

The clock keeps moving

Sixty Days registers the whole reminder ladder immediately, then keeps missing work visible instead of pretending a partial packet is complete.

Who this is for: a disaster survivor managing a dense decision letter while rebuilding daily life. No real survivor story or private data is used; every person, letter, and image in this demonstration is synthetic.

What this does not do

No legal or eligibility decision

It organizes quoted reasons and evidence tasks. It does not determine eligibility, predict acceptance, or replace legal advice.

No autonomous contact

Requests are prepared with blanks for the applicant to review and send. There is no contact, send, or submit endpoint.

No evidence authentication

Photo checks cover observable framing only. Ready for review never means genuine, sufficient, approved, or accepted.

The PDF is a draft. It is optional, not an agency form, not submitted, and intentionally keeps every missing item visible.

What this build proves

Quoted, not guessed

A reason is discarded unless its exact words occur in the transcription. Four recorded Gemini calls scored 20/20 extraction fields.

Screened, not adjudicated

Two recorded image calls scored 6/6 observable checks. The only outcomes are ready for applicant review, retake, or manual review-never “FEMA will accept this.”

Prepared, not sent

A utility or insurer request is drafted with blanks and tracked against the deadline. The applicant reviews and sends it; the service has no send route.

Grounded draft PDF

Every system-authored decision statement reproduces an exact letter quote. Missing evidence remains visibly listed in a partial packet.

Privacy by omission

Raw letters, photo bytes, transcriptions, and the applicant’s free-text statement are not persisted in the case record.

The whole clock exists now

Day 3 through day 58 wakes are registered at intake. A shorter printed deadline moves the packet and final alert safeguards earlier.

Research basis and limits

Deadline and supporting documents

FEMA’s current Individual Assistance quick reference says appeals must be submitted within 60 days of the decision-letter date and lists supporting documents such as receipts, bills, estimates, titles, and deeds.

Source: FEMA Individual Assistance appeals quick reference

The letter controls the ask

FEMA advises applicants to read the decision letter for the reason and requested documentation. Examples include insurance records, occupancy documents, and ownership records.

Source: FEMA, 8 Tips for Appealing FEMA’s Decision

Historical friction, not a product promise

GAO reported that common historical ineligibility reasons included insufficient damage and failure to provide supporting evidence. This motivates the workflow; it does not predict any applicant’s outcome.

Source: GAO-20-503

Evidence routes are source-specific

FEMA lists multiple ownership and occupancy records, while its insurance guide distinguishes settlement, denial, and proof of excluded or absent coverage. Sixty Days now routes those alternatives explicitly and treats photos as optional context, not proof of sufficiency.

Source: FEMA ownership and occupancy fact sheet
Source: FEMA insurance quick reference

Important: FEMA says supporting documents may be sent without a separate appeal letter or form. The generated PDF is therefore an optional organizing draft for applicant review, not a required agency form. A current FEMA IHP explainer also instructs applicants to put their application and disaster numbers on every submitted page, so this PDF repeats synthetic references in every footer for the applicant to verify. Source: FEMA IHP application, eligibility, registration, and appeals.

Questions a sceptic would ask

Does this file an appeal for me?

No. It has no submission endpoint. It prepares a reviewable draft and the applicant controls every external action.

Does ready for review mean FEMA will accept the photo?

No. It means only that requested observable features are visible. Authenticity, sufficiency, and agency acceptance are outside this system's scope.

Why use a simulated clock?

The demonstration compresses weeks into minutes. The same durable wake registration and claim path runs on wall-clock time; the UI labels every simulated advance.

Where does AI make a decision?

Gemini transcribes synthetic documents and reports observable image features. Deterministic code verifies exact quotes, routes requirements, schedules wakes, and preserves missing items.

Is any real survivor information used?

No. The fixtures are synthetic and unbranded. The persistence layer omits raw text, image bytes, real names, addresses, registration references, and applicant narrative; the API accepts only explicit demo-prefixed packet references.

How can a judge verify the rules?

The conformance report maps each public rule to an official source, implementation path, and passing test.
